Butler is a service robot for a hotel. A guest places an order. The robot collects the food or drink in the kitchen and leaves it at the room. The guest's money is held until the order is on the table. Then the payment is released. There is no front desk and no paper invoice. The dollars move because the work happened.

The first setting is a hotel, because hotels already run on repeatable jobs: fetch, carry, deliver. The same pattern can later cover other hotel tasks, and then other buildings. The hotel in the project today is a computer simulation. A real lobby comes after the demonstration is solid.

## What a job is

The robot is called servebot-1. It has an arm. In the simulation it serves a small set of rooms and stops at the door. It picks up an item at the counter, carries it, and sets it down. The kitchen is paid when the item is picked up. The rest of the guest's payment is released when the item is on the table, and that remainder is split between the operator, the maker of the robot, and a reserve kept for the robot itself. Those shares belong to the demonstration. A live hotel would set its own.

Three rules sit above the demo:

- The robot moves only after an order exists.
- Payment follows proof. Pickup pays the kitchen. Delivery releases the guest's money.
- The arm does its own work. A slow payment can hold the next order. It does not steer the arm.

## Why this matters now

Kenya's hotels sit inside a large tourism economy, and the work inside them is still done by hand.

In 2025, tourism earnings were about KSh 0.5 trillion. The country welcomed about 7.9 million tourists: 2.7 million from abroad and 5.2 million Kenyans travelling at home. Kenya Tourism Board and Invest Kenya describe a hospitality base of more than 900 accredited hotels and more than 45,000 rooms, with bed capacity up about 7.7 percent in 2024. The World Travel and Tourism Council's 2026 research puts travel and tourism at about 1.8 million jobs in Kenya in 2025, about 8.3 percent of employment.

On 27 September 2026, at World Tourism Day at the University of Eldoret, Tourism and Wildlife Cabinet Secretary Rebecca Miano said travellers now discover and book on a phone, and that Kenya has to meet them with digital tools. A hotel job that settles itself when the work is done is one of those tools. It starts in a simulated hotel a class can watch. A real property comes later, with the hotel's own agreement.

The aims, if a hotel partner agrees, are these:

- A demonstration a guest can understand: order, delivery, payment released because the tray arrived.
- A record of which robot did the job, so a hotel can check the work.
- A pattern that can move from room service to other repeated tasks in the same building.
- Staff who keep the jobs a robot should not take. The robot carries. People still host.

These are aims. The robot has not worked a real hotel shift.

## How the money and the record fit

Today the visit is a computer simulation of a hotel kitchen and a corridor. Under it, two plain systems do the trust work.

Digital dollars, through Circle, are held for the guest until the delivery is proved, on a test network called Arc. The robot has its own wallet for the kitchen payment. The keys stay off the robot, with a small local service. peaq keeps the public work record: which machine did the job, and that the pickup and the delivery happened. The robot already has a public identity on peaq. A pickup and a delivery have been written there. A test payment to the kitchen counter has gone through. The guest's full hold, from lock to release, is still being completed on that test network.

A school or a hotel pays in ordinary money. Nobody in the demonstration is asked to learn a new wallet to order a drink. The public record is there so the hotel, the guest, and the maker can check the job.

## What is real today

The simulation runs. The robot can collect an item and set it down. The work record for a pickup and a delivery exists. One kitchen payment has gone through on a test network. The full guest payment is still being finished. No hotel has hosted the robot.
