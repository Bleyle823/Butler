Butler is a service robot in a hotel. A guest orders food or a drink. The robot collects it in the kitchen and leaves it at the room. The guest's money stays held until the order is on the table. The kitchen is paid at pickup. The rest is released at delivery. A class can watch the whole job on a screen and see why the payment moved.

The first setting is a hotel. Kenya already has the rooms. Invest Kenya counts more than 900 accredited hotels and more than 45,000 rooms. The same pattern, a repeated task that pays itself when the work is proved, can later move to other hotel jobs and other buildings. Today the hotel is a computer simulation. The Centre would be looking at the learning use: a machine, a job, and a payment that waits for proof.

## What a class would see

A short lesson can follow one order.

- The guest asks for an item.
- The robot, servebot-1, collects it at the counter. The kitchen is paid.
- The robot sets the item down. The rest of the payment is released and split.
- A public record names the robot and the two moments: pickup and delivery.

Pupils see the job. They also see the limit: the arm does the carrying, and the payment follows. A delayed payment holds the next order. It leaves the arm alone.

## Why a classroom, and why now

In 2025, tourism earnings were about KSh 0.5 trillion, from about 7.9 million tourists. The World Travel and Tourism Council's 2026 research says travel and tourism supported about 1.8 million jobs in Kenya that year, about 8.3 percent of employment. Hotels are a large part of that work, and the jobs inside them are still learned by watching someone else carry a tray.

On 27 September 2026, Cabinet Secretary Rebecca Miano told the World Tourism Day gathering at the University of Eldoret that Kenya has to show its tourism on the digital tools travellers already use. Butler is a small, watchable piece of that: a hotel task a class can replay. Safari Live and the Ministry's own programmes are separate. This letter is my request to the Centre where I interned.

The aims are a lesson a teacher can run from a laptop, advice from the Centre on age and length, and, if the idea holds, a path toward incubation. A real hotel is a later step. The letter stays with the demonstration.

## How the record is kept

The visit on screen is a simulated kitchen and corridor. Digital dollars, through Circle on a test network called Arc, are the money. peaq is the public work record, and the robot already has an identity there. A pickup and a delivery have been written to that record. A test payment to the kitchen counter has gone through. The guest's full hold, from lock to release, is still being completed. A school pays the way it already pays. The wallet work stays with the demonstration, off the pupils' screen.
